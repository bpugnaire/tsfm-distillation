Learn: forecasting with foundation models

A short course built alongside this project. It explains the ideas behind every piece of tinycast, from "what is a forecast horizon" to "how do you distil a 200M-parameter model into 1M parameters and run it in a browser tab".

How each chapter works
  • Intuition first, in plain words, then Formally for the precise definition.
  • In our code shows the actual function from this repository (extracted at build time, so it is never out of date).
  • Try it widgets compute live in your browser. Move the sliders until the behaviour stops surprising you.
  • Pitfall boxes are mistakes we made or nearly made in this project.
  • Words with a dotted underline have a hover definition; all of them are in the glossary.
  • Each chapter ends with Check yourself questions. Try answering before opening them.

Learning path

Chapter 1 · 20 min
Forecasting fundamentals

Context, horizon, seasonality, autocorrelation, baselines, backtesting, leakage.
Chapter 2 · 30 min
Probabilistic forecasts and how to score them

Quantiles, the pinball loss, calibration vs sharpness, CRPS/WQL, MASE, error bars on scores.
Chapter 3 · 30 min
How time-series foundation models work

Scaling, patches vs bins, transformers, quantile heads, decoding, pretraining data.
Chapter 4 · 35 min
Knowledge distillation for forecasters

Soft targets for distributions, three losses, α, and which data to distil on (can it be noise?).
Chapter 5 · 20 min
Compression and the browser

Size vs latency, ONNX with preprocessing inside, int8 quantisation, parity, WASM.
Chapter 6 · 30 min
Running an ablation study

Reference recipe, one factor at a time, paired comparisons, sanity checks; worked example: our Stage A results.

Where this connects to the project

The course explains techniques. To see them applied: Draw runs the student live, Gallery and Results show the measured numbers, Lab notes record what happened and what surprised us, and Status shows what is done and what is waiting for a decision.

Prerequisites: comfortable with neural networks, gradient descent and basic probability (distributions, expectations). No prior forecasting knowledge needed.
