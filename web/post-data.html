From raw series to a million training windows

In short
  • A training example is a window: 512 past values the student reads, and 64 future steps the loss scores. The 64 steps carry the teacher's 13 quantiles.
  • The published student trained on 1,000,000 windows: half cut from real public series, half generated from random seeds. None comes from French electricity.
  • Each series is split by position: the first 70% for training, the next 15% for validation, the last 15% for test. A window belongs to the split that holds its whole target.
  • Window origins sit on a grid with a stride of 64, so no two targets share a step. Every dataset gets the same quota.
  • The teacher's labels sit in parquet shards next to a manifest, joined to the windows by a hashed id.

1. One training example

The student never sees a whole series. It sees fixed-size windows: 512 steps of context and a 64-step target. For distillation, the target also carries the teacher's quantiles, computed once and cached. With α = 1, the loss uses only those labels. The real future in the window is kept for validation and for ground-truth baselines.

context (input)real futureteacher's 10–90% and 30–70% rangesteacher's median

The student also standardises every window by itself (post 3), so a series in megawatts and one in passenger counts train side by side. Missing values stay NaN. The model gets a mask for them.

2. What the million windows are made of

Top: 500,000 windows cut from real series in public datasets, coloured by domain. Bottom: 500,000 synthetic windows, by generator.

Real series come from two openly licensed collections: Chronos's training datasets and Salesforce's GiftEvalPretrain (configs/data/gift_pretrain_v2.yaml). They cover traffic sensors, smart meters, wind farms, air quality, weather, web traffic and epidemiology. Every dataset gets the same quota. A dataset with fewer windows than its share hands the leftover to the others. Without a quota, the few datasets with thousands of long series would make up most of the corpus.

Windows per dataset. Most datasets hit the same cap (dashed line). The ones below it had fewer windows than their share. Energy and traffic still dominate because they have the most datasets, not the most series.

3. Cutting one series into windows

Here is one real series from the corpus, an hourly road-traffic sensor (). Public datasets end on different dates, so a single calendar cut-off is impossible. Each series is split by position instead: train for the first 70%, validation for the next 15%, test for the last 15%.

The rule that prevents leaks is about targets: a window belongs to a split only if its whole 64-step target lies inside that split. The context may reach back into earlier parts, because that is just the past. Origins sit on a grid with a stride equal to the horizon (64), shifted by a random offset per series. Two training targets therefore never share a step, while their contexts overlap by 448 of 512 steps:

Three consecutive grid slots of this series. Each row is one window: grey is its context, blue its target. The targets tile the series. Slot 3 is on the grid but was not drawn into the corpus: with a fixed quota per dataset, only part of each grid is used.

A window must have at least 90% of its target observed and 32 observed steps of history. Contexts shorter than 512 steps are left-padded with NaN. The id of a window is a hash of where it came from, so it is the same on every machine and in every run:

4. Synthetic series: free, unlimited, leak-free

The other half of the corpus is generated. Each window is a pure function of one integer seed, so nothing is stored: the corpus regenerates its series from the seed list. The seed ranges cannot collide: training seeds in [0, 500,000), validation seeds in [500,000, 1,000,000), and the gallery and test shapes from 1,000,000 up. A unit test checks the boundary between training and test seeds (tests/test_leakage.py).

    The synthetic tabs in section 1 show one window of each kind, with the teacher's labels.

    5. Keeping the test sets out

    Public data has one risk: the same series can appear in a training collection and in a benchmark. Before training, every candidate series is checked. The first 70% of the series is cut into 32-step chunks. Each chunk is z-normalised and hashed, and the hash is looked up in a set built from GIFT-Eval, fev, our electricity series and the held-out pedestrian counts. One matching chunk drops the whole series.

    The headline test, French electricity from RTE, is held out completely. It is never in the distillation corpus. Its split is calendar-based, and its test period starts after every model we compare was released:
